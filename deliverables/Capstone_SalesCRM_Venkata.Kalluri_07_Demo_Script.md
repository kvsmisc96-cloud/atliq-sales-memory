# AtliQ Sales Memory Assistant: 5-minute stakeholder demo script

**Audience:** AtliQ's founders (Bhavin, Dhaval, Karandeep) and the CEO.
**Your role:** a PM pitching a decision, not an engineer showing features.
**The ask at the end:** approve a 4-week beta with Dhaval.

Read the words in quotes aloud. The **Screen** lines say what to show. The timings add up to about 5 minutes at a normal speaking pace. The companion video `demo-walkthrough.mp4` follows these scenes in the same order and timing.

---

## 1. The hook (0:00 to 0:30)
**Screen:** title card, then the Daily brief.

"Last month AtliQ didn't lose deals because clients said no. We lost them because nobody wrote back. FinEdge is our biggest open deal at 95 thousand dollars. Their compliance team cleared us on 19 June and asked for a call. Twenty-one days later, nobody had replied. Across the pipeline, 405 thousand dollars is sitting in seven deals that have quietly stalled. I'm going to show you how we stop that."

## 2. Why it happens (0:30 to 1:00)
**Screen:** CRM fixes page, showing the "40/40 records with no follow-up date" box.

"This isn't a people problem. You're founders who sell, deliver and run the company. Conversations happen on the phone between meetings, and the CRM only knows what someone retypes later. Today, not one of our 40 CRM records has a follow-up date. Jay spends about two hours rebuilding the CRM before every pipeline review. We agreed to 'update the CRM same day' in April and again in July. It didn't stick, because it can't."

## 3. Scenario: Bhavin's morning (1:00 to 2:10)
**Screen:** Daily brief → Bhavin tab → Meridian card → open Sources → scroll the draft → Mark done.

"So here's Bhavin's morning. Instead of searching his inbox, he opens one list of at most seven things that need him today, in order.

At the top is Meridian. Their IT director asked two questions 24 days ago, and their deadline is 15 July. The assistant also says why it ranked this first: how long the client has waited, the deadline, and the deal value from the CRM.

Here's what makes it trustworthy. Click Sources, and you see the exact sentence from the client's email, marked as verified. Nothing appears on this list unless we can point to where it came from.

There's a draft reply ready. Notice the brackets: the assistant doesn't know our answer on HL7 integration, so it leaves a gap instead of making up a promise. Bhavin fills it in and sends it from his own email. Mark it done, and FinEdge moves up next. That's the 21-day email, caught."

## 4. Scenario: promises we forgot (2:10 to 2:40)
**Screen:** Karandeep tab → HealthFirst card → ByteBridge card.

"Switch to Karandeep. HealthFirst's board decides on our portal on 14 July, and we haven't spoken to them since April, so it reminds him to check in before the meeting. Further down is ByteBridge. On 12 May Karandeep promised two case studies 'by Friday'. That was 56 days ago. The assistant read that sentence and turned it into a task with a date and an owner."

## 5. Scenario: the CRM fixes itself, with your permission (2:40 to 3:20)
**Screen:** CRM fixes → Approve Harbor & Finch → scroll to duplicates → Audit log.

"The same reading keeps the CRM honest. Four leads wrote in and never reached the CRM. Harbor & Finch is a referral from FinEdge's own Suresh, and it's been sitting in an inbox. One click approves the new record. The assistant also spots that Meridian is in the CRM twice, and that Trustline said yes but still shows 'Proposal Sent'. Nothing changes until a person approves it, and every approval goes into this audit log with a name and a time."

## 6. Money we already earned (3:20 to 3:40)
**Screen:** Opportunities page.

"It also finds revenue we've already earned the right to. Acme, a happy client, mentioned they struggle with inventory forecasting. Quantia told us to check back in Q2 2026, and Q2 has passed. Both are now on the right person's list."

## 7. How it stays grounded and safe (3:40 to 4:15)
**Screen:** Evals page (12/12) → scroll to the guardrails.

"You should be asking: what if the AI gets it wrong? Three rules. First, every fact must be backed by a quote that we check word-for-word in the source. If I invent a '500k budget' quote, it gets rejected. Second, it never guesses: 19 deals with no stated value say 'Not stated'. Third, it never acts on its own. There is no send button, and no CRM change without a click. This page re-runs 12 known answers every time it loads, and all 12 pass today."

## 8. The business case and the ask (4:15 to 5:00)
**Screen:** closing card with the numbers.

"Here's the business case. It costs about 455 dollars a month to run, and the AI is only six and a half dollars of that. It saves Jay's two hours a review and the founders' admin time, so it pays back in about nine months, before counting a single revived deal. And if it helps us restart just one of those seven stalled deals, it has paid for itself many times over.

We'll measure one number: the share of follow-ups done on time. Today it's about one in six. The target is 80 percent in eight weeks.

My ask: a four-week beta with Dhaval, using the daily brief, the promise tracker and the waiting-on-us radar. If we don't move that number, we stop. Thank you."
